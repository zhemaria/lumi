The stories

These are “Cucu’s Stories”: true stories a great-grandfather wrote for his great-grandchildren. Each book opens in a new tab.

Israel and His Little Brother (opens in a new tab)

Israel y su hermanito

Israel can’t wait for his little brother… and finds out that jealous feelings can happen too, and how they go away.

Themes: family, jealousy, being a big brother.

👨‍👧 To read together
Kindergarten and Grade 1
📖 To read on your own
Grades 2 and 3

Longer paragraphs, about 65 words per page.

You Can Do It, Adrián! (opens in a new tab)

¡Tú puedes, Adrián!

Adrián starts Pre-K, and his big brother Israel teaches him that making mistakes is part of learning.

Themes: starting school, confidence, brothers.

👨‍👧 To read together
Pre-K and Kindergarten
📖 To read on your own
Grades 1 and 2

Short sentences, about 20 words per page.

Génesis, the Games Teacher (opens in a new tab)

Génesis, la maestra de los juegos

Cucu takes Israel and Adrián to Mexico, and Génesis teaches them the games of her town: jacks, ring games, spinning tops, the piñata…

Themes: family, Mexican traditions, learning through play.

👨‍👧 To read together
Pre-K and Kindergarten
📖 To read on your own
Grades 1 and 2

Short sentences, about 25 words per page, and a “Let’s play!” page in every chapter.

How to use each story

  • 🔊 Listen

    Every page can be heard in the narrator’s voice. Great for children who can’t read yet.

  • 🌎 Two languages

    One button switches the whole book from English to Spanish and back.

  • 🔠 Big letters

    The A+ button makes the text bigger for anyone who needs it.

  • 📑 Contents

    Goes straight to each chapter, to read a little every day.

  • ⌨️ Keyboard

    The arrow keys turn the pages; it works on computers, tablets and phones.

Which grade

  • To read together: an adult reads aloud (or plays the narration) while the child follows the pictures and talks about them.
  • To read on your own: the child can read it alone, with the narration as a support when they get stuck.
  • These are suggestions, not rules: every child has their own pace, and an “easier” story can be enjoyed at any age.

For families and teachers

Before reading

  • Look at the cover and the title together: what do you think it is about?
  • Ask if they have ever lived something similar.

While reading

  • Stop at a picture and ask the child to tell what they see.
  • Ask: “What do you think will happen next?”
  • Let the child read a sentence or a word they already know.

After reading

  • Ask them to retell the story in their own words, or draw their favorite part.
  • Read it again another day: rereading builds confidence and fluency.
  • Read the same story in the other language.
