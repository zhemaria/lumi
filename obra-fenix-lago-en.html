The Phoenix on the Lake

The Story of the Great Chicago Fire

A play by José M. Fernández

A play for the whole school, from Pre-K to 6th grade. The older students carry the dialogue and narration; the youngest are the animals, the fire, the wind and the river.

Act I · The Spark on DeKoven Street (October 8, 1871)

Stage direction: A lively fiddle or banjo tune plays. Upstage right, 4th and 5th graders stroll along a wooden plank sidewalk. Stage left is the barn: the Pre-K students rest like barnyard animals around Daisy the cow.

Narrator 16th

Chicago. October 8, 1871. It was one of the fastest-growing cities in the world. But it had a big danger: almost everything was made of wood! Houses, bridges, sidewalks, and even many streets.

Narrator 26th

It had hardly rained for weeks. The wood was as dry as kindling, and a strong wind was blowing from the southwest. One spark was all it would take.

Stage direction: The 2nd and 3rd grade winds cross upstage once, waving their ribbons, and exit.

Winds and embers (chorus)2nd–3rd

Whoosh, whoosh! We are the southwest wind!

Narrator 16th

That night, in a small barn on DeKoven Street, the fire began. No one knows for sure how. But there is a very famous legend... and this is how people tell it.

Stage direction: Mrs. O'Leary enters with a tin pail and a prop lantern with a safe LED light.

Mrs. Catherine O'Leary6th

(Patting Daisy) Hello, sweet girl. Time for your milking. The wind is so strong out there... I'll leave my lantern here in the corner, next to the hay, so I can see.

Stage direction: The Fire Chief steps forward and freezes the scene with a raised hand.

Fire Chief6th

(To the audience) Stop! Safety rule number one: never put a flame or anything hot near things that burn easily, like hay, paper or cloth. Safety rules exist for a reason.

Stage direction: The scene unfreezes in comic slow motion.

Narrator 26th

According to the legend, Daisy kicked her leg...

Stage direction: Daisy gently kicks a hind leg and knocks over the lantern.

Barnyard animals (chorus)Pre-K

Moo! Peep, peep! Watch out for the fire!

Stage direction: Red lights flicker. The kindergarten flames dance in, moving their arms to the beat.

Little Flames (chorus)K

Crackle, crackle, crack! We are the little flames!

Mrs. Catherine O'Leary6th

(Frightened) Fire! Fire in the barn! Help!

Act II · The Orderly Escape (October 8–10)

Stage direction: The 2nd and 3rd grade winds cross upstage in a wavy line with their ribbons, without running. Downstage, the 1st graders hold the long blue cloth of the Chicago River and move it in waves. The Pre-K animals, with their buddies, sit at stage right, on the lake shore.

Narrator 26th

The wind pushed the flames from block to block, leaping from roof to roof.

Winds and embers (chorus)2nd–3rd

Whoosh! Stronger, stronger!

Chicago River (chorus)1st

We are the Chicago River! The fire can't cross us!

Narrator 16th

But the wind carried burning pieces of wood through the air. The fire jumped the South Branch of the river... and then the main river, all the way to the North Side.

Stage direction: The flames move around the river cloth, guided by their teachers, and crouch down on the other side.

Little Flames (chorus)K

We jumped the river!

Stage direction: The 4th and 5th graders cross diagonally at a quick but orderly pace, carrying wheelbarrows, suitcases, blankets and books.

Resident 14th–5th

The fire has reached the North Side! Everyone to the shore of Lake Michigan!

Resident 24th–5th

(Stopping with a suitcase) I want to go back for my furniture and my letters!

Resident 34th–5th

(Gently taking her arm) Don't go back! Things can be replaced; lives cannot. Follow the route to the lake!

Stage direction: A 6th grader raises a cutout of the limestone Water Tower upstage.

Fire Chief6th

(Clearly, raising his lantern) Evacuation rule: walk, don't run; follow the route and keep the exits clear! Big kids, hold the little ones' hands!

Stage direction: 5th graders take the Pre-K students by the hand and bring them to the group.

Resident 44th–5th

(Pointing) Look at the Water Tower! Its stone walls are still standing! If we stay together and stay calm, we'll get through this!

Narrator 26th

The fire burned for more than a day. At last, on the night of October 9, it began to rain, and on October 10 the last flames went out.

Narrator 16th

Hundreds of people died, and about one hundred thousand were left without a home. More than seventeen thousand buildings burned.

Stage direction: The red light slowly fades to dark blue. The flames sit down, put out. Everyone is safe on the lake shore.

Act III · Rising from the Ashes

Stage direction: The backdrop panels turn to show the new Chicago of stone and brick. Shopkeepers and builders enter with wooden mallets, blueprints and wheelbarrows.

Architect6th

Give up? Never! A city isn't made only of wood and bricks. A city is made of the courage and the heart of its people.

Stage direction: The real estate agent nails a sign next to a little wooden shack.

Real estate agent4th–5th

My office burned down, but my sign tells the truth: “All gone but wife, children and energy.” Tomorrow I start again!

Resident 14th–5th

People across the country and around the world are sending us food, clothes and blankets. And from England, thousands of books are arriving for Chicago's first free public library!

Architect6th

This time we will build better. In the center of the city, no more wooden houses: only stone, brick and iron! And more firefighters, better prepared!

Narrator 16th

Chicago rebuilt so fast that the world was amazed. Years later, in 1885, one of the first skyscrapers with a metal skeleton was built here.

Narrator 26th

And in 1893, just twenty-two years after the fire, Chicago welcomed the world to the World's Fair, with its white palaces: the famous “White City.”

Stage direction: Two 6th graders unfold a Chicago flag made in class.

Narrator 16th

That's why the Chicago flag has four red stars. The second one remembers the Great Fire of 1871, and the third one, the World's Fair of 1893.

Act IV · Closing Reflection

Stage direction: All grades gather in order: the youngest in front, sitting; the oldest in back, standing.

Fire Chief6th

The fire left us lessons for a lifetime. First: respect safety rules, at home and at school. Second: be prepared: know the exits, keep the hallways clear and take fire drills seriously.

Mrs. Catherine O'Leary6th

And now let me tell you the truth about me. For many years, people blamed me and my cow. But in 1893, the reporter who made up the cow story admitted it wasn't true. And in 1997, the City of Chicago declared me innocent. No one knows for sure how the fire started.

Narrator 26th

So, before you believe a story, look for the evidence.

Mrs. Catherine O'Leary6th

And the greatest lesson our city learned: no fall lasts forever if we stand together.

4th grader4th

From the fire...

5th grader5th

...rose a Chicago that was stronger, safer and more beautiful.

All gradesAll

(Stepping forward) Always alert, always prepared! Together we can overcome anything!

Stage direction: Everyone sings the “Phoenix Chant” and bows by grade, from the youngest to the oldest.

Phoenix Chant
All grades

If there's smoke, don't run,
walk and tell a grown-up;
help the little ones,
and line up, line up!
Chicago fell,
Chicago rose:
like the Phoenix,
up it goes!
