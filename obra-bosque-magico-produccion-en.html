The author and his conditions

El bosque mágico is a play by Vicente Cañón Verdasco (© 2019), a playwright from Tarragona, Spain. His plays for children and young people are at www.obrasteatro.com.

In October 2026, the author gave Lumi permission to publish the interactive script, to translate it into English, and to add more villagers, fairies and elves so the whole school can take part, always keeping the original text.

The author offers the play free of charge for performance, on these conditions, which are accepted by using it:

  • His name, Vicente Cañón, must always appear as the author: before every performance and in everything that is published: posters, programs, social media, websites and videos.
  • The title of the play must not be changed: El bosque mágico. In English, it is presented as El bosque mágico (The Magical Forest).
  • The author would be very grateful to receive photos, links to videos, press clippings or reviews of each production, at his email: tente.ca2017@gmail.com. He would also like to receive a poster showing the title, his name, the school and the country.

Checklist for the author's conditions

  • The poster and the program say “El bosque mágico (The Magical Forest), by Vicente Cañón”. Use the Lumi poster.
  • The presenters say the author's name before the show begins (see jobs for non-actors).
  • Every post on social media, on the school website or in a video includes the title and the author's name.
  • Families have signed the photo permission (see the letter).
  • After the show, photos, video links and the poster are sent to the author.

At a glance
  • What it's about: near a magical forest there are three happy villages: the Village of Joy, the Village of Friendship and the Village of Generosity. The envious witches and wizards cast a curse on them and take away their happiness. The wise people don't know what to do, until some fairies and elves bring a plan: at night, while the witches and wizards are asleep, everyone will dance together in a circle around them. The dance breaks the curse, and the witches and wizards ask to be everyone's friends.
  • Values: joy, friendship and generosity, and their opposites: sadness, unfriendliness and selfishness. Problems are solved together, and someone who makes a mistake can change.
  • Running time: 20 to 25 minutes, depending on the music and the dances. The text is short: most of the play is movement, dancing and music.
  • Cast: the author wrote it for about 25 children aged 5 and 6, and says every group can grow. On Lumi we suggest it for the whole school, Pre-K to 6th grade, with the cast by grade.
  • Languages: the script is in Spanish and in English, with the same lines in the same order, so it can be performed in either language.

Cast by grade and numbers

The author wrote the play for about 25 children and noted that almost every part can be doubled: instead of four villagers per village there can be five, six or more, and the same goes for the witches, wizards, fairies and elves. That way the whole school takes part without changing the text.

  • When a line belongs to a group (“Witches”, “Fairies and Elves”, “All the villagers”), the whole group says it together.
  • When a line has a number (“Villager 1”, “Wise People 3 and 4”), it can be said by one child or by a small group.

Jobs for non-actors

Not everyone wants to or can speak on stage, and the show can't go on without these jobs. They are real parts: they appear in the program with each child's name.

  • 🎤 Presenters

    5th or 6th · 1 or 2 children

    They welcome the audience and announce the play and its author: “We present El bosque mágico, The Magical Forest, by Vicente Cañón.” The author asks for his name to be said before every performance.

  • 🔊 Sound

    4th to 6th · 2 children

    They play each track at the right moment, following the cue sheet.

  • 💡 Lights

    5th & 6th · 1 or 2 children

    Daylight for scenes 1 to 6, and blue nighttime light for the cave.

  • 🌳 Curtain and bushes

    5th & 6th · 2 to 4 children

    They open and close the curtain and check that the four bushes stand firmly.

  • 🧺 Props

    4th to 6th · 2 children

    They set out the cauldron and the ice creams on a table backstage and put them away afterward.

  • 📖 Prompter

    5th or 6th · 1 or 2 children

    They follow the script backstage and whisper a line to anyone who forgets it.

  • 🤝 Backstage buddies

    5th · 1 for every 2 or 3 little ones

    They take Pre-K and K children to their places, join them in the final dance and bring them back.

  • 🎨 Sets

    Any grade

    They paint the bushes, the cauldron and the backdrop of trees and mountains.

  • 👒 Costumes

    4th to 6th, with an adult

    They help the little ones put on wings, flower crowns, caps and hats.

  • 📰 Poster and program

    3rd to 6th · 2 to 4 children

    They prepare the poster and the program, with the title, the author and the names of the whole cast.

  • 🎟️ Front of house

    4th to 6th · 2 to 4 children

    They greet families, hand out programs and help people find a seat.

  • 📸 Photos and video

    6th, with an adult

    They film only the children whose families gave permission. These are the photos and videos sent to the author.

Props

  • Four cardboard bushes, sturdy and with their supports
  • Big cardboard pot or cauldron
  • Prop ice creams for the Village of Generosity
  • Painted backdrop with trees and mountains
  • All ten tracks in order and tested on the sound system
  • Colored tape on the floor: yellow, blue and red for the villages

↑ Close and back to top

Sets and props
  • Backdrop: mountains and trees painted on paper or cardboard, set apart from the curtain.
  • Four bushes cut out of cardboard and painted in several shades of green, with a cardboard back support folded into a triangle so they stand on their own. The witches and wizards hide behind them, so they need to be tall and wide.
  • A big pot or cauldron made of cardboard painted black, with fire and stones painted at the base, also with a back support. One witch and one wizard carry it in and out.
  • Prop ice creams made of foam sheets, cardboard or plastic, for the Village of Generosity.
  • The cave (scenes 7 and 8): no set is needed. The nighttime light and the witches and wizards lying down in the center are enough.

At the end of his play, the author includes photos of the bushes and the cauldron made for the original production, all of cardboard. The infographics in the play in pictures show how to make them.

Rehearsals

Weeks 1 to 3: each group in its classroom

  • Pre-K and K: 15-minute sessions. Waving to the audience when the narrator names their village, their village's dance, and the curse gestures: sad faces, sticking out tongues, not wanting to share.
  • 1st and 2nd: the entrance with music, the lines said together (“We have come to help you”, “On the count of three, we'll start to dance”) and the circle dance.
  • 3rd and 4th: the witches and wizards: the cauldron mime, the spell and the “choo-choo train”; the wise people: the worried walk and their dialogue.
  • 5th and 6th: the narrator's verses, calmly and in a clear voice, and the tech crew with the cue sheet.

Weeks 4 and 5: in blocks

  • Block A, the curse (scenes 1 to 4): witches and wizards with one village at a time.
  • Block B, the solution (scenes 5 and 6): wise people with fairies and elves.
  • Block C, the night and the dance (scenes 7 and 8): all the groups, with the backstage buddies.
  • Narrators: join each block to match their timing with the music.

Last week: dress rehearsals

  1. Mark with colored tape on the floor where each village sits; entrances and exits with music.
  2. The whole play with costumes, bushes, cauldron and ice creams, and the final bows.
No one has to memorize if they can't. Narrators can read on stage, with the script in a black folder. The little ones say their lines together, and a buddy can whisper the line to them just before. In the script, each child can choose a part, see only their own lines and listen to them read aloud.

In the classroom

The author recommends that, before rehearsals begin, classes explore the play's values and their opposites: joy and sadness, friendship and unfriendliness, generosity and selfishness.

  • Pre-K and K

    Faces and gestures. Show “happy” and “sad”, “friends” and “cross”, “sharing” and “not sharing” with face and body. That is exactly what they will do on stage.

    Share something among everyone, such as paper ice creams, and talk about how sharing feels.

  • 1st and 2nd

    How can we help? The fairies and elves don't fight: they come up with a plan. What can we do when a classmate is sad or angry?

    Practice the circle and count “one, two and three” all together.

  • 3rd and 4th

    Envy and second chances. Why are the witches and wizards envious? At the end they ask to be friends and everyone says yes: why is forgiving important?

    The wise people can't find the answer on their own: when is it good to ask for help?

  • 5th and 6th

    Verse and rhyme. Find the rhymes in the narration (“wood” and “good”, “dance” and “chance”) and read them aloud, keeping the beat.

    Compare the English with the original Spanish: how was “colorín, colorado” translated?
